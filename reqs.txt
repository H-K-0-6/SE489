 Introduction  
The rapid growth of digital platforms has significantly transformed the way individuals and 
businesses interact, trade, and collaborate. In particular, e -commerce systems have become 
essential tools for connecting sellers with a wider audience while offeri ng customers 
convenient, secure, and efficient purchasing experiences. Alongside traditional online stores, 
auction -based marketplaces have gained increasing popularity due to their ability to create 
competitive pricing environments and enhance user engage ment. These technologies are 
especially valuable for small -scale producers and artisan cooperatives seeking broader market 
reach and sustainable business opportunities.  
This project presents the design and analysis of an E-Commerce Website for a Local Artisan 
Cooperative , developed to support both fixed -price product sales and real -time online 
auctions. The system aims to provide a unified digital marketplace where customers can 
browse products, place orders, participate in auctions, and track their purchases, while 
artis ans can manage their shops, products, orders, and sales performance through dedicated 
dashboards. The platform is designed to ensure security, usability, scalability, and compliance 
with modern e -commerce standards, making it suitable for both local and in ternational users.  
The report follows a Software Engineering  approach, applying the DevOps -based Software 
Development Life Cycle (SDLC)  to emphasize continuous integration, continuous 
deployment, and ongoing feedback. Security and compliance considerations are treated as 
core concerns throughout every development phase, reflecting best practices in modern 
system design. Functional, non -functional, business, and regulatory requirements are 
systematically identified and mapped to ensure that the system satisfies stakeholder needs 
while maintaining high performance and reliability.  
By combining e -commerce functionalities, auction mechanisms, and cooperative values within 
a single platform, this project demonstrates how software engineering principles can be 
applied to build a robust, user -centric, and secure digital marketplace. The report documents 
the system’s requirements, architecture, diagrams, and design decisions, providing a 
comprehensive blueprint for the development of a real -world cooperative online marketplace.  
 
Scenario  
When a new customer visits the cooperative’s website, they can register by entering their full 
name, a valid email address, and a secure password. The system ensures that the provided 
email follows the correct format before completing registration, and all  passwords are safely 
encrypted before being stored. Artisans, on the other hand, can create accounts by not only 
submitting their email and password but also by providing shop details such as their biography, 
profile picture, and shop name. Once registere d, both customers and artisans can log in to the 
system using their credentials. For security and flexibility, the platform also offers logout 
functionality and password recovery through email verification in case users forget their login 
details.  4 | P a g e  
 After logging in, customers are welcomed with access to the product catalogue, where they can 
browse and search for products using keywords, artisan names, or product categories like  
painting, jewellery, pottery, or textiles. Each product page displays clear details, including the 
product name, description, price, multiple images, and artisan profile information. Customers 
can add items they like to their shopping cart, review their cart, make a djustments such as 
changing item quantities or removing product s, and instantly see the updated total cost. Once 
ready to make a purchase, they follow a guided checkout process where they provide a 
shipping address and payment details, after which the system generates an order confirmation 
and sends a confirmation ema il to the customer. From their user account, customers can also 
track the status of their orders — from pending to shipped and finally delivered.  
Artisans experience the platform through a dedicated dashboard that allows them to manage 
their profiles and products effectively. They can add new products with descriptions and 
images, as well as edit or remove existing listings. When a customer places a n order, the 
artisan receives a notification and is able to update the order’s status, ensuring customers are 
aware of shipping progress. Alongside product management, artisans can also review their 
complete sales history and examine performance reports to  understand their business growth.  
The system manages all orders with unique identifiers, enabling both customers and artisans to 
view detailed order histories. While relevant transaction details are stored to maintain accurate 
records, sensitive payment data is never kept within the platfo rm. Payments are processed 
securely through integrated payment gateways, and customers receive email confirmations 
once payments are completed successfully.  
For a more dynamic marketplace, artisans also have the option to list their products in 
auctions. To create an auction, artisans provide details such as the product description, a 
starting bid, and the auction’s start and end times. Customers can then plac e bids, with the 
system enforcing that any new bid must be higher than the current highest one. The auction 
interface displays the leading bid and bidder details in real time, and customers receive 
notifications if they are outbid. At the scheduled end tim e, the auction closes automatically, 
and both the winning bidder and artisan are notified. The winning customer is then guided to 
complete the payment for the item. The marketplace separates fixed -price products from 
active and upcoming auctions for easier  browsing.  
To enhance user experience, the cooperative’s website includes extra features such as product 
and artisan reviews, where customers can leave ratings and feedback. They can also create and 
maintain personalized wishlists. For international reach, the platfo rm supports multiple 
languages and currencies, making accessibility easier for customers worldwide. Finally, 
administrators have access to a dedicated dashboard from which they can manage all platform 
activities, including users, artisans, products, orders , and auctions, ensuring smooth and 
secure operations for the entire cooperative.

 Literature Review  
The literature review highlights several platforms that closely reflect the concept of an online 
auction website for artworks, with each system offering a distinct model that can be leveraged.  
 
eBay  is one of the most well -known platforms that relies on an open bidding system. It allows 
users to list items and run auctions where buyers from around the world can place bids. The 
platform is highly flexible and user -friendly, making it suitable for  a wide variety of products, 
including artworks. It also provides useful features such as setting a starting price, defining 
auction duration, and offering a “Buy It Now” option, giving sellers greater control over how they 
present their items and maximize  sales opportunities.  
 
In contrast,  Catawiki operates as a curated auction platform where listings are reviewed by 
experts before being published. It focuses on unique items such as artworks and rare 
collectibles, ensuring quality and authenticity. This approach builds trust am ong users and 
attracts buyers who are specifically looking for distinctive and verified pieces, making it a strong 
model for specialized art auction platforms.  
 
On the other hand, Sotheby’s  represents a high -end and professional model in the art auction 
industry. As a historic auction house, it combines traditional auction practices with modern 
digital access, allowing users to participate in auctions both online and in person. The platform 
focuses on premium, high -value artworks and offers advanced features such as live -streamed 
auctions and professional art evaluations.  
 
Together, these platforms demonstrate how auction systems can range from simple and 
accessible to highly specialized and professional, while sharing the common goal of creating 
competitive bidding environments, increasing the value of artworks, and connect ing sellers 
with a global audience.

 dedicated security and compliance track runs alongside every phase, reinforcing that security

The first phase, Plan , captures all requirements engineering activities, including backlog 
creation, epic definition, stakeholder sign -off, risk and security review, and sprint milestone 
planning. This phase lays the foundation for the platform's domain areas, covering user 
registration and authentication, product management, auction mechanics, order processing, 
and the administrator dashboard.  
The second phase, Develop , represents the coding and collaboration workflow. Developers 
work on isolated Git feature branches, submit pull requests for peer review, and run static code 
analysis and dependency vulnerability scans before any code is merged. This is particularly 
impo rtant given the multi -role nature of the system, which includes customers, artisans, and 
administrators with distinct access levels and permissions.  
The third phase, Build (CI) , is the Continuous Integration stage, where every code merge 
triggers an automated pipeline that compiles the application, runs linting and type checks, 
executes unit and API tests, and produces a versioned Docker image stored in an artefact 
registry. A b uild status badge provides immediate visibility into pipeline health.  
The fourth phase, Test , encompasses a comprehensive quality assurance process. It includes 
integration tests, end -to-end tests covering critical user journeys such as authentication and 
checkout, dynamic application security testing (DAST) and penetration testing, load and 
auct ion stress tests to validate the real -time bidding engine under high concurrency, and 
accessibility testing to support the platform's multilingual and international audience.  
The fifth phase, Release , acts as a formal gate before any code reaches production. It involves 
promotion to a staging environment, an administrative approval gate, semantic versioning, the 
generation of release notes and changelogs, and the preparation of a rollback plan in case  a 
deployment needs to be reversed.  
The sixth phase, Deploy (CD) , is the Continuous Delivery stage. The platform is deployed using 
blue -green and canary release strategies to minimise downtime — a critical consideration for a 
live auction system where interruptions would directly impact active bids. Infrastructure as 
Code (IaC) manages environment provisioning, environment -specific configuration is injected 
at runtime, and automated smoke tests verify the deployment immediately after it completes.  
The seventh phase, Operate and Monitor , covers the live observability of the running system. It 
includes centralised metrics collection and logging, uptime alerting, dedicated monitoring of 
the auction engine, payment gateway log auditing, and an on -call rotation to ensure rapid 
incident respo nse.  
The eighth and final phase, Feedback and Iterate , closes the DevOps loop. User behaviour 
analytics, bug and issue triage, sprint retrospectives, artisan and customer satisfaction scores 
(NPS), and performance benchmarks are all collected and fed back into the planning phase, 
driving continuous improveme nt across the platform.  8 | P a g e

Stakeholder s 
1. Customers  Registered users who browse the product catalogue, place orders, participate in 
auctions, make payments, submit reviews, manage wishlists, and track order delivery status 
through their accounts.  
2. Artisans  Registered sellers who create and manage shop profiles, list fixed -price and auction 
products, receive and update order statuses, view sales history, and monitor performance 
reports through their dedicated dashboard.  
3. System Administrators  Internal operators who oversee the entire platform through the 
admin dashboard, managing users, artisans, products, orders, and auctions to ensure smooth, 
secure, and compliant platform operations.  
4. Authentication System  A technical stakeholder responsible for validating user credentials, 
enforcing email format rules, encrypting and storing passwords securely, managing session 
tokens, and supporting password recovery flows via email verification.  
5. Email Service Provider  An external service that handles all outgoing platform emails, 
including registration confirmations, order confirmations, payment receipts, password recovery 
links, auction outbid notifications, and auction win notifications.  
6. Payment Gateway  An external financial service that securely processes customer payments 
at checkout and after auction wins, ensuring that no sensitive payment data is stored within the 
platform itself.  
7. Database System  A technical stakeholder responsible for persisting all platform data, 
including user profiles, product listings, order records, auction bids, reviews, wishlists, and 
transaction histories, while enforcing data integrity and access control.  
8. Shipping Providers  External logistics partners who handle the physical delivery of purchased 
products, supplying tracking information that feeds into the order status updates visible to both 
customers and artisans.  
9. Regulatory Authorities  External governmental and legal bodies whose data protection, 
consumer rights, financial compliance, and e -commerce regulations the platform must adhere 
to, particularly given its international and multi -currency reach.  
10. Translation / Localisation Services  External or integrated services responsible for 
translating platform content and adapting currency displays to support the multilingual and 
multi -currency requirements of the platform's international customer base.  9 | P a g e  
 11. Non -registered Visitors  Unauthenticated users who can browse the public -facing areas of 
the platform, such as the product catalogue and artisan profiles, but who cannot place orders, 
bid in auctions, or access account -specific features without registering.  
12. Developers / Maintenance Team  Internal technical staff responsible for building, testing, 
deploying, and maintaining the platform, resolving bugs, releasing updates, monitoring system 
health, and ensuring the reliability and security of all platform components over time.  
Functional/Non -functional Requirements  
1. Functional Requirements (FR)  
• FR1 (User Registration):  The system shall provide two distinct sign -up forms: one for 
Customers (Name, Email, Password) and one for Artisans (Email, Password, Shop 
Name, 500 -character Bio, and Profile Image upload).  
• FR2 (Email Validation):  The registration module must verify that email inputs contain 
an "@" symbol and a valid domain extension before allowing form submission.  
• FR3 (Authentication & Recovery):  The system shall implement session -based login 
and a password reset feature that sends a unique, 15 -minute expiring link to the user’s 
verified email.  
• FR4 (Product Browsing):  The catalog shall support keyword search and filtering by four 
predefined categories: Painting, Jewellery, Pottery, and Textiles.  
• FR5 (Cart Management):  The cart shall calculate a running total of all items, including 
quantity adjustments, and persist data for logged -in users for up to 30 days.  
• FR6 (Order Processing):  Upon checkout completion, the system shall generate a 10 -
digit unique Order ID and trigger an automated confirmation email within 60 seconds.  
2. Non -Functional Requirements (NFR)  
• NFR1 (Usability):  New users should be able to complete a purchase from the 
homepage in 5 clicks or fewer without external assistance.  
• NFR2 (Availability):  The platform shall maintain a 99.9% uptime, allowing no more than 
43 minutes of unplanned downtime per month.  
• NFR3 (Performance):  All pages must achieve a Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) of under 
2.5 seconds on a standard 4G connection.  
• NFR4 (Scalability):  The system must handle a 300% spike in traffic (up to 1,000 
concurrent users) during holiday sales without a drop in response time.  
• NFR5 (Data Security):  All user data at rest must be encrypted using AES -256 standards.  
3. User Requirements (UR)  10 | P a g e  
 • UR1 (Purchase Journey):  Customers require a "One -Click" add -to-cart feature directly 
from search results to streamline the buying process.  
• UR2 (Artisan Management):  Artisans require a visual "Sales Performance" chart on 
their dashboard showing monthly revenue trends.  
• UR3 (Secure Access):  Users require visual confirmation (e.g., a lock icon) and Multi -
Factor Authentication (MFA) options for high -value transactions.  
• UR4 (Order Tracking):  Customers require a real -time status bar (Pending > Shipped > 
Delivered) accessible via their "My Orders" page.  
• UR5 (Admin Control):  Administrators require the ability to "Shadow" or "Suspend" 
accounts that violate the cooperative’s terms of service within two clicks.  
4. System Requirements (SR)  
• SR1 (Web Accessibility):  The system must be accessible via any modern browser 
(Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge) and support screen readers (WCAG 2.1 Level AA 
compliance).  
• SR2 (Payment Integration):  The system shall integrate with external APIs 
(Stripe/PayPal) to process Visa, Mastercard, and Amex payments.  
• SR3 (Data Storage):  The system shall utilize a relational database (e.g., PostgreSQL) to 
maintain ACID -compliant records of all transactions.  
• SR4 (Notification Engine):  The system shall integrate with an SMTP server to deliver 
100% of system -generated alerts to users' inboxes.  
• SR5 (Concurrency):  The backend must support at least 200 simultaneous database 
writes per second during high -volume auction bidding.  
5. Business Requirements (BR)  
• BR1 (Artisan Promotion):  The homepage must reserve at least 40% of its "Above the 
Fold" space for rotating "Artisan of the Week" features.  
• BR2 (Marketplace Trust):  The platform shall implement a "Verified Artisan" badge for 
sellers who have successfully completed 50+ orders with a 4.5 -star rating.  
• BR3 (Revenue Growth):  The system must support a 10% commission fee automatically 
deducted from each non -auction sale to fund cooperative operations.  
• BR4 (Global Reach):  The platform shall implement SEO -friendly URLs for all products 
to increase organic search visibility by 20% annually.  
• BR5 (Transaction Efficiency):  Total checkout time from "Cart" to "Confirmation" should 
average less than 90 seconds for returning users.  
6. Interface Requirements (IR)  11 | P a g e  
 • IR1 (Web UI):  The interface shall use a consistent color palette and typography (Design 
System) across both Customer and Artisan views.  
• IR2 (Dashboards):  The Artisan Dashboard must provide a distinct "Inventory Low" alert 
when product stock falls below 3 units.  
• IR3 (Mobile Design):  The UI must be fully responsive, with all touch targets (buttons) 
being at least 44x44 pixels on mobile devices.  
• IR4 (Payment UI):  The payment interface must be hosted via a secure, PCI -compliant 
iframe to ensure no sensitive card data is handled by the local UI.  
• IR5 (Email UI):  All system emails must be formatted in responsive HTML/CSS to ensure 
readability on both mobile and desktop mail clients.  
7. Performance Requirements (PR)  
• PR1 (Multi -User Support):  The system must support up to 5,000 registered users with 
500 active sessions simultaneously.  
• PR2 (Transaction Speed):  Payment gateway handshakes must be completed within 5 
seconds for 99% of all transactions.  
• PR3 (Search Latency):  Search results for a database of 10,000 products must be 
returned in under 800ms.  
• PR4 (Auction Real -Time):  Bid updates must be pushed to all active viewers via 
WebSockets within 300ms of the bid being placed.  
• PR5 (Peak Load):  During the final 5 minutes of an auction, the system shall prioritize bid 
processing over non -essential page requests.  
8. Security Requirements (SEC)  
• SEC1 (Encryption):  All passwords must be hashed using the Argon2 or BCrypt algorithm 
with a unique salt per user.  
• SEC2 (Authorization):  The system shall use Role -Based Access Control (RBAC) to 
ensure Artisans cannot access Admin dashboards or other Artisans' sales data.  
• SEC3 (Zero -Storage Policy):  The platform must not store CVV codes or full Credit Card 
Numbers in its local database or logs.  
• SEC4 (Brute Force Protection):  The system shall lock any account for 30 minutes after 
5 consecutive failed login attempts.  
• SEC5 (HTTPS):  All data transmission must be forced over TLS 1.3 (HTTPS) with an A+ 
rating on SSL Labs.  
9. Regulatory / Compliance Requirements (CR)  12 | P a g e  
 • CR1 (GDPR/Data Laws):  The system must include a "Cookie Consent" banner and a 
"Right to be Forgotten" button within the user settings.  
• CR2 (Privacy):  A clearly linked Privacy Policy must be accessible from every page 
footer, detailing how user data is utilized.  
• CR3 (PCI -DSS):  The system must comply with PCI -DSS Level 4 requirements for secure 
online payment processing.  
• CR4 (E -Commerce Standards):  The system shall provide automated digital tax invoices 
for every purchase to comply with local trade regulations.  
• CR5 (Audit Trails):  All financial transactions must be logged in a read -only audit file 
accessible only by the System Administrator.  
10. Operational Requirements (OR)  
• OR1 (Maintenance):  Scheduled maintenance windows must be communicated to 
users 24 hours in advance and occur during low -traffic hours (2 AM – 4 AM).  
• OR2 (Backup):  The system shall perform full database backups every 24 hours and 
transaction log backups every 15 minutes to an off -site cloud bucket.  
• OR3 (Monitoring):  The system shall use an automated monitoring tool (e.g., 
Datadog/New Relic) to alert admins via SMS if CPU usage exceeds 90%.  
• OR4 (Support Logs):  The system must maintain a log of all customer support tickets 
and their resolution times for quarterly review.  
• OR5 (Recovery):  The Disaster Recovery Plan must guarantee a Recovery Point 
Objective (RPO) of 15 minutes.  
11. Domain Requirements (DR)  
• DR1 (Categorization):  Every product uploaded by an artisan must be assigned to at 
least one of the four domain -specific categories (Painting, Jewellery, Pottery, Textiles).  
• DR2 (Auction Logic):  Auctions must have a mandatory "Start Price" and a "End 
Timestamp" set at the time of creation.  
• DR3 (Bid Validation):  The system shall reject any bid that does not exceed the current 
highest bid by at least the "Minimum Increment" defined for that auction.  
• DR4 (Order IDs):  Order identifiers must follow a standard prefix (e.g., COOP -2024 -
XXXXX) for easy identification by the cooperative.  
• DR5 (Multi -Currency):  The system shall update currency exchange rates via a third -
party API every 12 hours.  
12. Inverse Requirements (IRV)  13 | P a g e  
 • IRV1 (Payment Storage):  The system shall not allow the local database to save raw 
credit card data under any circumstances.  
• IRV2 (Invalid Email):  The system shall not proceed with registration if the email 
validation check returns a false or "disposable email" status.  
• IRV3 (Illegal Bidding):  The system shall not accept a bid from the artisan who owns the 
product being auctioned (shill bidding prevention).  
• IRV4 (Cross -Account Access):  The system shall not allow a Customer to view the 
"Artisan Dashboard" unless they have upgraded their account type.  
• IRV5 (Data Deletion):  The system shall not allow the deletion of an order record if it is 
currently in "Shipped" or "Pending" status.  
13. Design Requirements (DRQ)  
• DRQ1 (UI/UX):  The design shall use "High Contrast" settings (contrast ratio 4.5:1) for all 
text to assist visually impaired users.  
• DRQ2 (Architecture):  The system shall use a Microservices or Modular Monolith 
architecture to allow the "Auction" module to be updated without taking down the 
"Storefront."  
• DRQ3 (Scalable Assets):  All images must be served in WebP format and automatically 
resized based on the user's screen resolution.  
• DRQ4 (Navigation):  The "Breadcrumb" navigation style must be present on all product 
and auction pages to orient the user.  
• DRQ5 (Layout):  The system must maintain a "Sticky Header" on desktop views to keep 
the Search bar and Cart icon visible at all times.  
14. Implementation Requirements (IRQ)  
• IRQ1 (Coding Standards):  All code must pass a static analysis tool (e.g., SonarQube) 
with zero "Critical" or "Blocker" vulnerabilities before deployment.  
• IRQ2 (Database):  The database implementation must include "Foreign Key" constraints 
to prevent orphaned order records.  
• IRQ3 (API Integration):  External API calls must have a 10 -second timeout to prevent the 
system from hanging if a third -party service is slow.  
• IRQ4 (Data Encryption):  Personal Identifiable Information (PII) such as phone numbers 
and addresses must be encrypted at the database column level.  
• IRQ5 (Testing):  The system must undergo "Load Testing" to simulate 10,000 users and 
"Penetration Testing" to identify security flaws before the official launch.  14 | P a g e

 Context Diag ram  
 
 
This diagram is a Context Diagram (Level 0 Data Flow Diagram)  for the Cooperative Online 
Marketplace System . It illustrates the high -level interactions between the core system and its 
external entities, defining the boundaries and data flows of the platform.  
 
Overview of Entities and Data Flows  
1. Artisan (External Entity)  
The Artisan is the primary provider of goods on the platform. The data flows between the Artisan 
and the System include:  
• Inbound to System:  The Artisan provides registration details, manages product listings 
(creation/editing), initiates new auctions, and submits order status updates (e.g., 
changing status to "shipped").  
• Outbound from System:  The system provides the Artisan with essential information, 
including product details, updates on active orders, auction alerts (such as new bids), 
and system confirmations.  
2. Customer (External Entity)  
The Customer is the end -user purchasing goods. Their interactions are focused on the 
storefront:  
17 | P a g e  
 • Inbound to System:  The Customer submits data for registration and login, executes 
product searches, performs cart actions (add/remove), completes the checkout 
process, places bids on auctions, and leaves reviews or wishlist updates.  
• Outbound from System:  The "Receive Info" flow represents the system delivering 
catalog data, order status tracking, bid results, and confirmation emails back to the 
user.  
3. Admin (External Entity)  
The Admin represents the governing body of the cooperative, ensuring smooth operations:  
• Inbound to System:  The Admin sends commands to manage the overall platform, 
including user accounts, product moderation, order dispute resolution, and auction 
oversight.  
• Outbound from System:  The system provides the Admin with comprehensive sales 
reports, system logs, and notification alerts regarding platform performance and 
activity.  18 | P a g e

 UI Design Analysis: Cooperative Online Marketplace  
The provided interface showcases a high -fidelity landing page for an artisan -focused digital 
marketplace. The design employs a modular, section -based layout that uses distinct color 
blocking to guide the user through different functional zones of the platf orm.  
1. Visual Hierarchy and Color Strategy  
The design utilizes a bold, high -contrast palette to define different content areas:  
• Hero and Header:  A clean, transparent navigation bar sits atop a large -scale artistic 
banner. The primary call -to-action (CTA) is a high -visibility green button labeled "BET 
NOW," emphasizing the auction aspect of the platform.  
• Featured Art Section:  Set against a deep red background, this area uses white product 
cards to create high legibility.  
• Artist Directory:  A warm golden -orange background differentiates the "Top Artists" 
section from product listings, shifting the focus from items to individuals.  
• Recruitment Zone:  A cerulean blue block serves as a dedicated portal for new artisans, 
utilizing a split -screen layout that balances text with high -detail photography of a 
craftsperson.  
2. Component Design and Functional Elements  
The interface relies heavily on standardized "Card" components to present complex data 
concisely:  
• Auction Cards:  Each card features a "LIVE" status indicator, artisan attribution (avatar 
and name), and real -time bidding data (number of bids, current price, and a countdown 
timer). Interactive icons for "Like," "Cart," and "Quick View" are grouped at the top of the 
image area.  
• Artisan Profile Cards:  These emphasize the human element of the cooperative. They 
include professional portraiture, the artisan’s name, their specialty, and integrated 
social media links for further engagement.  
• Navigation and Utilities:  The header contains standard e -commerce utilities, including 
a search bar, shopping cart with a count indicator, and user authentication links 
(Login/Register).  
3. User Experience (UX) Considerations  
• Clarity of Purpose:  By placing auction -specific data (current bid and time remaining) 
directly on the product cards, the design reduces the cognitive load on the user, 
allowing them to assess multiple auctions without leaving the main page.  25 | P a g e  
 • Responsive Architecture:  The modular card -based system is inherently designed for 
responsiveness, suggesting a seamless transition to mobile views where cards would 
stack vertically.  
• Conversion Optimization:  Clear, bordered buttons like "Learn More" and "Join Now" 
provide unmistakable pathways for both customers and potential artisans to move 
deeper into the system.  
4. Information Architecture  
The page follows a logical "AIDA" (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) marketing funnel:  
1. Attention:  Large -scale art in the Hero section.  
2. Interest:  Featured live auctions and new arrivals.  
3. Desire:  Building trust through the "Top Artists" profiles.  
4. Action:  The "Become an Artisan" recruitment section at the bottom.  
 
The interface successfully merges a commercial auction environment with a community -driven 
cooperative feel. The use of vibrant color blocking prevents the long -scrolling page from feeling 
repetitive, while the consistent card typography ensures that techn ical data—such as bid 
amounts and timers —remains the focal point of the marketplace experience.  
Architecture  of The System using MVC  
 
This diagram illustrates the MVC (Model -View -Controller) Architectural Pattern  tailored for 
the Cooperative Marketplace system. It defines the high -level organization of the platform into 
three specialized layers to ensure a clean separation of concerns:  
1. View Layer (UI Screens)  
26 | P a g e  
 This layer represents the User Interface . It contains specific screens such as the AuctionView , 
ProductCatalogView , and ShoppingCartView . It is responsible for presenting data to the user 
and is noted as being "viewed by the actor."  
2. Controller Layer (Logic Handlers)  
Acting as the system's "brain," this layer handles user input and orchestrates the flow of the 
application. It includes specialized controllers like the AuctionController  and AuthController . 
As indicated by the "receives actor events"  note, it intercepts actions from the user (such as 
placing a bid) and determines the appropriate response.  
3. Model Layer (Data & Entities)  
This layer manages the business logic and data  of the marketplace. It groups related entities 
from the class diagram —such as User/Artisan , Product/Category , and Auction/Bid . It maintains 
the state of the system and ensures data integrity.  
 
Core Interaction Flow:  
• Action : The User/Actor  triggers events that the Controller  receives.  
• Logic : The Controller  sends a command to modify  the Model  (e.g., saving a new bid).  
• Update : The Controller  coordinates the UI by creating and updating  the View . 
• Feedback : The Model  ensures the UI remains accurate by notifying the View  about 
state changes (e.g., updating the highest bid in real -time).
